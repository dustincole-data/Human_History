Four ways to make it move

Same 72 real, cited cut-outs. The photographs are never touched in any of them — colour and light live in the space around the objects. Nothing is picked. Open each one and scroll; these are built to be moved through, so a still tells you almost nothing.

1 · DEPTH fly through it

The objects are in a volume, not on a page, and the scroll flies the camera through it. Each era's light is a real light in real air — haze, shafts, drifting dust, bloom. The deep past is one object alone in the dark; the present is a crowd you pass straight through.

native scroll · direction B made real

2 · GRAVITY it breaks down

Everything breaks. Each object falls in, shatters on a ground that never moves, and the pieces keep breaking until there is nothing left the earth cannot take. The fall is the scrollbar: stop halfway and it hangs there. Three ways to come apart: SHATTER, CRUMBLE, SIFT.

native scroll · height is a function of scroll

3 · MATERIAL a living ground

The ground is not a colour, it is a running simulation — wet clay, oxidised copper, ground pigment, ink in water, molten iron, plasma. It moves under the cursor and churns when you scroll fast. Objects sit on top, crisp and untouched.

native scroll · closest to the built version

4 · ATLAS steer through it

Not a scroll at all. The whole span is a place: drag to turn it, wheel to fly in. From far out you see the actual shape — a thin thread that thickens into a cloud. Fly in and single objects name themselves.

no scrollbar · breaks the native-scroll rule on purpose
